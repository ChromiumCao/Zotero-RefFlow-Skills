2. Experimental evidence on interrupted sitting

Short activity breaks have been examined in populations with different metabolic profiles. A randomized crossover study in healthy, normal-weight adults found that interrupting prolonged sitting with brief activity reduced postprandial glycemia [1]. Another experiment reported that breaking up sitting with light- or moderate-intensity walking reduced postprandial glucose and insulin responses [2]. Despite addressing related questions, these studies used distinct samples and protocols; their effects should not be treated as interchangeable.

Studies in clinical populations provide another perspective. In people with type 2 diabetes, Duvivier and colleagues compared prolonged sitting, a structured exercise regimen, and a regimen involving more standing and light-intensity walking. The Sit Less condition improved 24-hour glucose measures and insulin sensitivity relative to prolonged sitting [3]. The intervention involved substantially more activity than a few brief two-minute breaks, so it would be inaccurate to describe these results as proof that a minimal dose has the same effect.
